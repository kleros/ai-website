01 Protected Payment

Payments agents can call, protected by default.

Agents don't open a browser. They call an endpoint, get a priced list, pay, and receive the goods. The money moves on agent-native rails and is held rather than pushed, and Kleros decides what happens when the delivery doesn't match the receipt.

  1. One call to browseA single request returns your complete catalogue with prices attached.
  2. Agentic PaymentAny agent-native rail, with the funds held instead of pushed. The transaction can be contested by either party if something goes wrong.
  3. Arbitration, not a coin flipA contested payment doesn't reach a support queue or a default refund. It enters an arbitration process you designed: your policy, your evidence, your escalation tiers.

02 Verification

Verification anyone can read, and anyone can challenge.

A list creator writes the standard. Anyone can apply against it. What comes out is a certification held on chain, plus a JSON object your code reads in one call: verified or not, and everything that was verified.

  1. The creator sets the standardAcceptance criteria and required fields are published up front. Open source, versioned, no admin key.
  2. Requesters applyAn applicant submits their entry with a deposit. It is certified unless someone challenges it inside the window.
  3. Anyone can contest itIncluding a consumer who got burned downstream. Challenges go to jurors, who rule on whether to confirm or revoke the verified status.

A shared standard is also the cheap path. Without one, every agent burns tokens, money, and time re-investigating who it can safely deal with. With one, it reads a single field and moves on.

fully on chain · read over REST or GraphQL · one field decides access: status = Registered

03 Disputes

Keep your payment rails. Add dispute resolution.

Not every dispute sits next to a transaction or an agent-native payment method. A refund requested after a support thread. A flight cancelled months after it was billed. Bring the case, not the money.

  1. Smart contractFunds stay locked and release themselves when the ruling lands. No operator in the loop.
  2. Recognition of jurisdictionNo chain integration at all. You commit up front to enforce the ruling; cases go over an API and come back decided. Fast, because enforcement was agreed in advance.
  3. Review sized to the caseA dispute over a few cents shouldn't cost what a fifty thousand dollar one does. The reviewer scales with the stakes.
Cost structure

A deeper review is literally more model: longer context, more tokens, more passes, and more wall-clock time before a ruling comes back. So the tier is drawn from the amount at stake. A two cent dispute never pays for a two hundred dollar review.

Solidity or REST and webhook · appeals escalate to a larger panel
